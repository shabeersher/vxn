Without Automation:

Marketing automation doesn't replace marketers—it amplifies their impact.

Customer Journey Orchestration

What Are Customer Journeys?
Automated, multi-step campaigns that guide contacts through personalized experiences based on their behavior.

Journey Components:

Triggers (Entry Conditions):

  • Form submission
  • Email link clicked
  • Event registration
  • Website visit
  • CRM record change

Actions:

  • Send email
  • Send SMS
  • Create task for sales rep
  • Update lead score
  • Add to segment
  • Wait period (hours, days, weeks)

Decisions (If/Then Logic):

  • Did they open email? → Yes: Send follow-up / No: Send reminder
  • Is lead score > 50? → Yes: Notify sales / No: Continue nurturing
  • Downloaded asset? → Yes: Send related content / No: Wait

Example Journey: Webinar Registration

  1. Trigger: Contact registers for webinar
  2. Action: Send confirmation email with calendar invite
  3. Wait: 24 hours
  4. Action: Send reminder email with webinar link
  5. Wait: Until webinar date
  6. Action: Send thank-you email with recording
  7. Decision: Did they attend?
    • Yes → Send follow-up content offer
    • No → Send recording with missed benefits
  8. Wait: 2 days
  9. Decision: Did they engage with content?
    • Yes → Create sales task for follow-up
    • No → Continue nurturing journey

Real-Time Journey Execution:
Contacts progress through journeys based on their actions, not fixed schedules.

Journey Analytics:

  • How many contacts are at each stage?
  • Where are drop-offs occurring?
  • Which paths convert best?
  • Journey completion rates

Lead Scoring and Qualification

What Is Lead Scoring?
Assigning point values to leads based on characteristics and behaviors, identifying sales-ready leads.

Scoring Models:

Demographic Scoring:

  • Company size: Enterprise (+20), SMB (+10)
  • Industry: Target industry (+15), Other (+5)
  • Job title: Decision maker (+20), Influencer (+10)
  • Location: Target geography (+10)

Behavioral Scoring:

  • Opened email: +2
  • Clicked link: +5
  • Downloaded whitepaper: +10
  • Attended webinar: +15
  • Visited pricing page: +20
  • Requested demo: +50

Engagement Scoring:

  • Frequency of engagement
  • Recency of engagement
  • Consistency over time

Negative Scoring:

  • Unsubscribed: -50
  • Competitor: -20
  • Job role not relevant: -10

Lead Grades:
Combine scores into grades:

  • A: 80+ points (Hot, sales-ready)
  • B: 50-79 points (Warm, continue nurturing)
  • C: 25-49 points (Cool, early-stage nurturing)
  • D: <25 points (Cold, minimal engagement)

Automatic Sales Handoff:
When lead reaches "A" grade → Automatically create opportunity and assign to sales rep.

Segmentation

What Is Segmentation?
Grouping contacts based on shared characteristics for targeted campaigns.

Dynamic Segments (Auto-Updating):

  • "All contacts in manufacturing industry with title containing 'director' who downloaded guide in last 30 days"
  • Contacts automatically join/leave as criteria change

Static Segments (Fixed):

  • "2024 Trade Show Attendees"
  • "Webinar Participants - Q4 Campaign"
  • Manual additions/removals

Segmentation Criteria:

  • Demographics (industry, company size, location)
  • Firmographics (revenue, employees, growth stage)
  • Behavioral (email engagement, website visits, content downloads)
  • Lifecycle stage (awareness, consideration, decision)
  • Purchase history
  • CRM data (opportunity stage, account owner)

Personalized Campaigns:
Send different messages to different segments:

  • Enterprise prospects: Focus on scalability and ROI
  • SMB prospects: Emphasize affordability and ease of use
  • Manufacturing: Industry-specific use cases
  • Healthcare: Compliance and security messaging

Account-Based Marketing (ABM)

What Is ABM?
Targeting specific high-value accounts with coordinated, personalized campaigns across all stakeholders.

Traditional Marketing: Cast wide net, attract many leads, qualify, narrow down.

ABM: Identify target accounts first, create personalized campaigns for each account.

ABM in Dynamics 365 Marketing:

Account Lists:

  • Define target account lists
  • Research and profile accounts
  • Identify buying committee members

Coordinated Campaigns:

  • Personalized content for each account
  • Multi-touch across email, events, ads, social
  • Synchronized outreach across buying committee

Account-Level Engagement Tracking:

  • Aggregate engagement across all contacts at account
  • Account-level scoring
  • Buying committee engagement visualization

Sales and Marketing Alignment:

  • Account plans shared between marketing and sales
  • Coordinated outreach
  • Real-time visibility into account engagement

Example ABM Campaign:

Target Account: ABC Manufacturing ($5M opportunity)

Buying Committee:

  • CFO (Budget authority)
  • CIO (Technical decision)
  • VP Operations (End user)
  • IT Director (Influencer)

Campaign:

  1. Awareness: Personalized video message to each stakeholder
  2. Education: Role-specific content (CFO: ROI calculator, CIO: Technical whitepaper, VP Ops: Case study)
  3. Engagement: Invite to exclusive executive roundtable
  4. Consideration: Custom demo addressing their specific challenges
  5. Decision: Personalized proposal and executive-level meetings

Track engagement across all touchpoints and stakeholders.

ROI Measurement

  • Software costs
  • Content creation
  • Advertising spend
  • Event costs
  • Agency fees

Revenue Attribution:

  • Opportunities influenced by marketing
  • Closed deals from marketing campaigns

ROI Calculation:

Marketing ROI = (Revenue from Marketing - Marketing Costs) / Marketing Costs × 100%

Example:

  • Marketing Costs: $50,000
  • Revenue from Marketing: $200,000
  • ROI: ($200,000 - $50,000) / $50,000 × 100% = 300% ROI

Common Marketing Automation Mistakes

Mistake #1: Buying a List
Purchased lists have low engagement, damage sender reputation, and violate privacy laws.

Solution: Build your list organically through content, events, and opt-in campaigns.

Mistake #2: Sending Too Frequently
Bombarding contacts leads to unsubscribes and spam complaints.

Solution: Respect engagement levels. Reduce frequency for less-engaged contacts.

Mistake #3: Generic Messaging
One-size-fits-all emails get ignored.

Solution: Segment and personalize. Tailor messages to audience needs.

Mistake #4: Ignoring Mobile
50%+ of emails are opened on mobile devices.

Solution: Use responsive email templates. Test on multiple devices.

Mistake #5: No Clear CTA
Emails without clear calls-to-action don't drive action.

Solution: Every email should have one primary CTA. Make it obvious.

Mistake #6: Not Testing
Sending campaigns without testing leads to embarrassing errors.

Solution: Test emails across email clients, check links, review personalization tokens.

Mistake #7: Set It and Forget It
Launching campaigns without monitoring performance.

Solution: Regularly review analytics, optimize based on results, iterate continuously.

Marketing Automation Best Practices

1. Define Clear Goals
What are you trying to achieve?

  • Generate X qualified leads per month
  • Achieve Y% email open rate
  • Drive Z event registrations
  • Generate $X in pipeline

2. Know Your Audience
Build detailed buyer personas:

  • Demographics and firmographics
  • Pain points and challenges
  • Information needs
  • Preferred communication channels
  • Buying process

3. Map Customer Journeys
Understand how customers progress:

  • Awareness → Consideration → Decision → Post-Purchase
  • Content and messaging for each stage
  • Touchpoints and channels

4. Create Valuable Content
Content is the fuel for automation:

  • Educational blog posts
  • In-depth guides and ebooks
  • Case studies and success stories
  • Webinars and videos
  • Templates and tools

5. Nurture, Don't Spam
Provide value in every communication:

  • Solve problems
  • Educate and inform
  • Build trust over time
  • Respect preferences

6. Measure and Optimize
Continuous improvement:

  • Track key metrics
  • Analyze what works
  • Test new approaches
  • Scale winners, eliminate losers

7. Align with Sales
Marketing and sales must work together:

  • Agree on lead definitions (MQL, SQL)
  • Establish SLAs for lead follow-up
  • Share feedback loops
  • Collaborate on messaging
